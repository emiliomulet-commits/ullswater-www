Investment Readiness

Prepare your company and your proposal to meet the standards of EU evaluators, the EIB and EIF-backed investors.

Book a readiness reviewTry the free AI pre-check

Securing European funding, whether a grant, a loan or an equity investment, takes more than a brilliant idea. It is a highly competitive process in which your project is judged on its intrinsic merit and also on its presentation, strategic alignment and professional credibility.

European institutions such as the Commission's Directorates-General, the European Investment Bank (EIB) and the European Investment Fund (EIF) are stewards of public capital. They need to see a project that is innovative, viable, well managed and closely aligned with their objectives.

Presenting your project the right way is not about appearances. It is about showing a deep understanding of the funding landscape and presenting your project with the clarity and professionalism that inspire confidence. A poorly structured proposal, a misaligned business case or a missed formality can sink even a groundbreaking project. Our advisory services make sure your company is ready to meet these standards from the very first interaction.

What funders need to see

A robust, bankable project with a well-developed business plan. The EIB and its financial intermediaries need to be convinced of your project's long-term financial sustainability and of its fit with strategic priorities such as the green and digital transitions. An EIB-backed loan often has a signalling effect that makes it easier to attract other private investors.

Common stumbling blocks we help you avoid

  • A vague problem definition or a weak business case.
  • No real-world validation or market traction.
  • No clear path to profitability and scale.
  • Poorly prepared investor documents and a weak pitch.
Falling short in any of these areas signals risk to the funding body. Our role is to help you reduce that risk by building a compelling, professional case tailored to the type of funding you are seeking.Emilio Mulet, Managing Partner, Ullswater Investments

A visual guide

Keys to an excellent proposal

EU evaluators and investment managers review many applications against tight deadlines. They look for proposals that are clear, coherent and easy to assess.

Phase 1

Strategic preparation

Before you write, lay the foundations.

Internal analysis
Assess your strengths, resources and innovation capacity.
Opportunity watch
Monitor the official portals and shortlist the calls that best match your strategy.
Consortium building
Find reliable, complementary partners when the call requires a consortium. The Enterprise Europe Network (EEN) can help.
Phase 2

The three pillars of evaluation

Evaluators look for clarity and coherence: strategic alignment, viability and professional execution.

Pillar 1

Strategic alignment

Does your project directly contribute to the stated policy goals of the funding programme?

Pillar 2

Project viability

Is your plan realistic, your budget coherent and your business model sound?

Pillar 3

Professional execution

Is the application flawless, compliant with every rule and presented with absolute clarity?

Phase 3

Execution and submission

The quality of the submission is the evaluators' first impression.

Clear writing
Use clear, concise and persuasive language, free of jargon and errors.
External review
Ask experts for feedback. National Contact Points (NCPs) offer free guidance; in Spain, see the official Horizon Europe portal.
Current framework
Calls for 2026–2027 follow the Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026–2027. Horizon Europe ends in 2027 and its successor, FP10 (2028–2034), is still being negotiated, so plan multi-year funding roadmaps with both in mind.
Timely submission
Submit through the EU Funding & Tenders Portal well before the deadline. Horizon Europe calls close at 17:00 Brussels time and late proposals are not accepted.
